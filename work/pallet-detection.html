Georgia-Pacific · Automation & Robotics R&D · 2019

Autonomous forklift pallet detection and orientation

At Georgia-Pacific in 2019, I built a pallet detection and orientation system for autonomous forklifts from the ground up, using a stereo depth camera (an Intel RealSense-era sensor) mounted on the vehicle. Since no suitable training data existed, I collected it myself from manual forklift operation, then trained a ResNet-backbone object detector from scratch to localize pallets in the RGB stream.

To get orientation, I used the detector's bounding box to index into the corresponding region of the depth image, estimating the pallet's pose from that RGB-D fusion rather than RGB alone. That pose estimate fed directly into trajectory planning, giving the forklift what it needed to autonomously approach and pick up a pallet without a person in the loop — a full zero-to-one build, from data collection through a model driving real vehicle behavior.
